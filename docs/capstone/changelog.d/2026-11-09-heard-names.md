# What the voice actually said, not just how clearly

- **The name check now shows what came out.** After a render, the **YouTube** section lists the names the voice said its own way, with the letters it was heard saying, how many of its mentions came out that way — `“eltarel” · 21 of 21` — and the IPA you asked for beside them. Until now the check could only read confidence, which says how clearly a name was said, never which name it was: a name mispronounced the same way in all 48 mentions scores as well as one said right, so nothing flagged it.
- **It costs nothing to compute.** The timing model already decodes every window to check the audio against the transcript at all, and that decode over one word's own frames is the letters it heard there. Slopify now keeps them on a word whenever they are not the word itself — any difference, because "Loth" for "Lolth" is one letter out and is exactly the mistake worth catching. On a real two-hour narration about 5% of words carry it (855 of 18,789), and `subtitles.json` grows by that much.
- **Spelling that is only spelling is folded away.** The model writes what it hears and has no idea how a name is spelled: on a real two-hour narration it wrote “creeg” for Kreeg, “bell” for Bel, “tier” for Tyr and “drist” for Drizzt, every one of them the name said correctly. All twelve such names are folded out and none is reported, while `Lolth` heard as “loth” and `Elturel` heard as “eltarel” are kept.
- **One word never decides.** A name is listed only where one other sound covers six in ten of at least three mentions, so a single mention the model misheard stays quiet. Run over one real two-hour video, that names 3 of the 150 names it tracks and leaves the other 147 alone, `Elturel` among them: it came out as “eltarel” 33 times of 145, which is not most of them.
- **The letters are a second model listening, not a verdict,** and the page says so: read them, and if one is wrong, change that name's IPA in **Name pronunciations** and narrate again.
- **An older video says so rather than passing.** Captions timed before this release carry none of it, and an empty answer there means nobody listened, not that the names came out right. The check reports that plainly and says making the captions again fills it in for nothing but time.
- **A word spelled unlike how it sounds can still be named.** `Heartlands` heard as “hartlands” is the voice reading it correctly, and no fold fixes that without a pronouncing dictionary. The letters are shown so you can see it for what it is.
- **Agents see it too:** `check_narration_names` gains `saidAs`, `ipa` and `different` per name, and `listened` for whether the timing was ever listened to at all.
