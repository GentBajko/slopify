# Does this video say the names right?

- **Before the narration is paid for, one line names what the voice would invent.** The project page's **Narration** section says which names this article uses that nothing pronounces — not its own Pronunciation Glossary, not your **Name pronunciations** list, not the pooled copy of the other projects' glossaries — and **Every mention** opens the table: each name, how often it is said as written and as a possessive, proper names first. It is the cheapest moment to settle a name, because adding it to the list costs nothing and the voice has not been paid yet. The line never holds the run up.
- **It catches the names a glossary looks like it covers and doesn't.** A two-word term is never matched by one of its words said alone, so an article that lists `Belwar Dissengulp` and then says `Belwar` 22 times is named. So are possessives and hyphenated names in a project narrated under the old pronunciation rules, which never wrapped either: a project is judged by the rules it was voiced under.
- **After the render, before the upload, the same line sits on the YouTube section.** It says two things from files the project already has. What the voice was actually sent, counted from the voice script itself — a name with no IPA in front of it there was guessed, whatever the glossary says. And how the finished narration was heard saying each name, read from the per-word confidence the subtitle timing already carries: the mean, and the spread across its mentions.
- **The spread is the signal, not a low score.** A rare name is heard poorly even when it is said perfectly, so only a name whose own mentions disagree with each other is flagged: eight mentions or more, and a 10th-to-90th-percentile spread of 0.30 or more. Measured on 30 finished videos, that flags `Do'Urden` in both videos that say it and leaves `Gygax` alone in all 26 that do.
- **Nothing is listened to again, re-aligned, changed or charged for,** and neither line blocks the run or the upload. Ordinary English words are left out: a one-word name the same text also writes in lower case is a word here, not a name, which is why the pooled copy's `Hall` and `Quick` stay quiet while `Torm` does not.
- **Agents get both:** `check_pronunciations` before the narration and `check_narration_names` after the render.
