# Videos come out AV1 on an NVIDIA card, and a template can pick its codec

- **By default the long video is now AV1, encoded on the NVIDIA card, wherever the card can encode AV1.** That holds whatever Settings → Production defaults → Video encoder says. Measured on a real 3-minute stretch of a 1080p video, slow pan and zoom over dense engraving, the card's AV1 is about 44% of the size of today's H.264 file for the same picture (about 94.4 VMAF against it). Without a card, or on one that can't encode AV1, the video is made exactly as before, H.264 through the Video encoder setting, and nothing is said about it: that is the ordinary way, not a fallback. Should the card's AV1 fail mid-render, the video is made again the old way.
- **Video codec is a new setting in the Video section of Create, the template editor and Edit settings: Automatic, H.264, HEVC or AV1.** Automatic is the default and is what a project without the setting gets. H.264 keeps every video H.264 as before and never AV1. HEVC and AV1 make a much smaller file: AV1 on the processor (SVT-AV1) is about 29% of the H.264 size, AV1 on the card about 44%, HEVC on the card about 59%. HEVC on the processor (x265) starts at CRF 26 and has not been measured yet.
- **The explicit choices combine with the Video encoder.** On the processor, HEVC is x265 from Slopify's own ffmpeg, and AV1 is SVT-AV1 from an ffmpeg on the computer (its `ffmpeg` on the PATH or `/usr/bin/ffmpeg`), checked once with a tiny test encode, as the NVIDIA encoder is. On the NVIDIA card they are its HEVC and AV1 encoders, at the same highest-quality settings as its H.264.
- **A video is never failed for its codec.** An explicit AV1 on a processor without SVT-AV1 is made as H.264, and a card that can't encode the chosen codec makes H.264 itself; the project's Video part says so and where to change it. A card that stops mid-render hands HEVC to x265 and AV1 to H.264.
- **Only the finished video changes.** Clips that are decoded again under burned-in captions stay H.264, since nobody watches them; every clip that is joined by copying opens on its own keyframe, as before. Shorts and the style preview stay H.264, for the services shorts go to.
- **No existing video turns outdated.** Automatic and H.264 add nothing to a video's fingerprint, so a finished video stays current and comes out AV1 the next time it is made for another reason; choosing HEVC or AV1 makes only the video again.
