# A template can make its videos in HEVC or AV1

- **Video codec is a new setting in the Video section of Create, the template editor and Edit settings: H.264, HEVC or AV1.** H.264 stays the default and works everywhere. HEVC and AV1 make a much smaller file for the same picture. Measured on a real 3-minute stretch of a 1080p video, slow pan and zoom over dense engraving, every file scoring about 94.4 VMAF against today's H.264 one: AV1 on the processor (SVT-AV1) is about 29% of the size, AV1 on an NVIDIA card about 44%, HEVC on an NVIDIA card about 59%. HEVC on the processor (x265) starts at CRF 26 and has not been measured yet.
- **It combines with Settings → Production defaults → Video encoder.** On the processor, HEVC is x265 from Slopify's own ffmpeg, and AV1 is SVT-AV1 from an ffmpeg on the computer (its `ffmpeg` on the PATH or `/usr/bin/ffmpeg`), checked once with a tiny test encode, as the NVIDIA encoder is. On the NVIDIA card they are its HEVC and AV1 encoders, at the same highest-quality settings as its H.264.
- **A video is never failed for its codec.** AV1 on a processor without SVT-AV1 is made as H.264, and a card that can't encode the codec makes H.264 itself; the project's Video part says so and where to change it. A card that stops mid-render hands HEVC to x265 and AV1 to H.264.
- **Only the finished video changes.** Clips that are decoded again under burned-in captions stay H.264, since nobody watches them; every clip that is joined by copying opens on its own keyframe, as before. Shorts and the style preview stay H.264, for the services shorts go to.
- **No existing video turns outdated.** A project without the setting, or set to H.264, plans exactly the fingerprints it did; switching to HEVC or AV1 makes only the video again.
