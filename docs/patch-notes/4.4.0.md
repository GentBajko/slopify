# Slopify 4.4.0

Released 13 November 2026.

Pans and zooms now move evenly, a video with captions in the picture is made in a little over half the time, videos keep the colours of their pictures, and opening a finished project no longer stalls the app.

## Pans and zooms no longer shake

- **A slow pan now moves by the same amount every frame.** Since images are held for their share of the narration, a shot runs about half a minute and a pan advances under half a pixel a frame. The picture could only be placed on whole pixels, so it stood still for a frame and then jumped: measured on a finished video, steps between nothing and 0.73 of a pixel where 0.46 was meant. They now measure 0.39 to 0.50, and what is left is the encoder's own noise.
- **A zoom holds its centre.** It wandered by up to 0.4 of a pixel from one frame to the next; it now moves by under 0.05.
- **The picture is placed between pixels,** in one pass at the frame's own size, instead of being enlarged four times and stepped. Fine detail is as sharp as before, within three percent either way.
- **Files are about three percent larger at the same quality setting,** because a picture that really moves every frame gives the encoder more to describe than one that stands still every other frame.
- **A still larger than the frame keeps its detail,** as before: it is sampled from a larger copy than a still with no more pixels than the frame, and each is kept away from the one size where fine detail would flicker.

## A video with captions is encoded once

- **Where each image is shown once, every clip now draws its own captions.** Until now the clips were encoded, joined, and the whole video encoded a second time to draw the captions over it, so every frame was compressed twice, the second time from a copy the first had already softened. Each frame is now sampled from its still, captioned and encoded in one go, and the join only copies.
- **It takes a little over half the time.** Measured on the first 24 images (11.5 minutes) of a real project on a 16-core processor: 83 seconds, against 155 in 4.3.5. The picture scores slightly higher against a lossless render (93.8 VMAF against 93.2 for two encodes), since it is compressed once, and the file is 1% larger.
- **A video whose same clips come round again keeps the two encodes,** because there they are the cheaper way: twelve stills that revolve for two hours at a fixed number of seconds each are a few minutes of distinct clips, and decoding a clip costs less than sampling its still again. Slopify picks per video: clips draw their own captions when the distinct clips are a third of the video or more, which a video whose shots follow the narration always is, since no two shots are the same length.
- **Changing one image or one caption no longer re-encodes the video.** Clips that draw their own captions are kept with them for the next render, so only the clips that changed are made again; before, any change sent the whole video through the caption encode. A two-hour video with 240 images took 14.7 minutes to make, sound included, and 1.7 minutes to make again with nothing changed.
- **Captions land on the exact frame.** A caption that starts at the very moment a frame is shown is now on that frame; a rounding used to put a few captions in a hundred one frame late. Checked frame by frame on that 11.5-minute render: 5 frames of 20,655 differ, each by that one frame.
- **Clip encoding: Fast now means the same for every video with captions,** about a sixth less time for a slightly softer picture. For clips that draw their own captions it is x264's faster preset (69 seconds against 83, 1.2 VMAF less, a sixth smaller); the two-encode route keeps what Fast always did there.

## Videos keep the colours of their pictures

- **Every video is now BT.709 and says so.** Pictures were turned into video with the older BT.601 formula and the file carried no colour labels, while players and YouTube read an unlabelled HD video as BT.709. The result was a slight warm cast on every video: reds a few steps too strong, blues a few too weak.
- **This covers every still that goes into a video:** plain, zooming and panning pictures, the four colour grades, portraits beside captions and the atmosphere layers. A clip that is already video keeps its own colours and only gets the label.
- **Coloured captions and chapter cards match as well,** with the NVIDIA encoder too. White text never changed.
- **Videos already made stay as they are;** make one again to get the corrected colours. The first render after updating encodes every clip afresh rather than reusing saved ones, since their colours differ.

## Opening a finished project no longer stalls the app

- **A finished project's page opens without freezing everything else.** Working out what Keep outputs only would free read every saved version once for each of the project's files: about two and a half seconds on a two-hour video with 600 files, during which no page got an answer, and it ran again on every visit and every return to the window. It now reads them once, in about two hundredths of a second, and answers the same.
- **Settings → Storage answers in a couple of seconds,** where it took over twenty with 170 projects. It asked the same question of every project in turn.
- **A running step no longer keeps the page asking for the whole project.** Each progress report, up to two a second, made the open page fetch everything it shows again, the saved version included (megabytes on a finished project). A report now fetches the meter and the cost, once a second at most, and the rest only every few seconds.
- **Coming back to the window no longer fetches the saved version and the name checks again.** Live events and saves already keep those fresh. Everything else is still asked again on return, which is how the page learns what the extension did in YouTube Studio.
- **Reading a saved version takes about half the time,** for every page of a project and every step of a run: its files are looked up once, not one at a time.
