# Slopify 4.3.0

Released 9 November 2026.

4.2.0 could tell you how *clearly* the voice said a name. It could not tell you *which* name it said. This release closes that, using something the computer was already working out and throwing away.

## What the voice actually said

- **The YouTube section now lists the names that came out as another sound,** with the letters the timing model heard, how many of its mentions came out that way, and the IPA you asked for beside them — `Lolth · 28 said · “loth” · 25 of 28 · you asked for /lɒlθ/`. This is the one thing confidence can never show: a name mispronounced the same way in every mention scores as well as one said right, so the spread in 4.2.0 saw nothing.
- **It costs nothing and listens to nothing twice.** Timing the captions already decodes every window to check the audio against the transcript at all; that same decode over one word's own frames is the letters the model heard there. Slopify now keeps them wherever they are not the word itself. On a two-hour narration about five words in a hundred carry them.
- **Spelling that is only spelling is folded away first.** The model writes what it hears and has no idea how a name is spelled: on a real two-hour narration it wrote “creeg” for Kreeg, “bell” for Bel, “tier” for Tyr, “drist” for Drizzt and “fandelver” for Phandelver — every one of them the name said correctly. All of those are folded out and none is reported, while a name heard as a sound it does not spell is kept.
- **One mention never decides.** A name is listed only where one other sound covers six in ten of at least three mentions. On that same video it names three of the hundred and fifty names the video tracks, and leaves the rest alone — `Elturel` among them, heard as “elterel” thirty-three times of a hundred and forty-five, which is real and is not most of them.
- **The letters are a second model listening, not a verdict,** and the page says so. A name whose spelling is nothing like its sound can be listed although the voice read it correctly; read the letters against the IPA, and if what came out is wrong, change that name's IPA in **Name pronunciations** and narrate again.

## An older video says so instead of passing

Captions timed before this release carry none of this, and a name with nothing against it there was never listened to — which is not the same as a name that came out right. The check says that plainly rather than calling the video clean. Making its captions again fills it in and costs nothing but time.

## For agents

- `check_narration_names` gains `saidAs` and `ipa` on every name, `different` for the names most of whose mentions came out as another sound, and `listened` for whether the timing was ever listened to at all.
